7.1 What is Investment — How It Differs from Saving
Saving = protecting money. Investment = growing money. This is the basic difference. When you keep money in a savings account, it grows by 3-4%, but inflation is 5-6% — so in real terms, your money is decreasing! Through investment, you beat inflation and create wealth. "Put money to work, do not work for money" — this is the core philosophy of investing.

7.2 SIP — Systematic Investment Plan (Complete Guide)
What is SIP? Invest a fixed amount every month automatically — it can start from ₹100! Many people do not know this. The magic of SIP lies in "rupee cost averaging" — whether the market goes up or down, you invest the same amount every month. When the market is down, you get more units (cheaper!). When the market is up, you get fewer units (more expensive!). The average cost remains balanced.
Rupee Cost Averaging Example:
Month NAV Invested Units Bought
1 ₹20 ₹1,000 50.0
2 ₹15 ₹1,000 66.7
3 ₹25 ₹1,000 40.0
4 ₹20 ₹1,000 50.0
Total — ₹4,000 206.7
Average NAV = ₹20. But average cost = ₹4,000 / 206.7 = ₹19.35. Even after market fluctuation, your cost is less than ₹20! This is the power of SIP.
Step-by-Step: How to Start a SIP:
Step 1: Complete KYC (PAN + Aadhaar + selfie). Online 10 minutes.
Step 2: Download app (Groww / Zerodha Kite / Paytm Money / ET Money — all four reliable, SEBI registered).
Step 3: Verify account — online in 2-3 minutes.
Step 4: Choose fund — Nifty 50 Index Fund is best for beginners.
Step 5: Set amount — ₹100 minimum, ₹500-₹1,000 recommended.
Step 6: Turn ON auto-debit — fix date (2-3 days after salary). Done!
What is NAV: Net Asset Value — the price of one mutual fund unit. Just like share price, NAV is for mutual fund units. ₹20 NAV = 1 unit costs ₹20. NAV changes daily.
What are Units: Mutual funds do not have "shares", they have "units". Invested ₹1,000, NAV ₹20 = got 50 units. NAV became ₹25 = your 50 units × ₹25 = ₹1,250 value.
Entry/Exit Load: Most funds no longer have any load (stopped by SEBI). But check — some funds have 1% exit load on redemption within 1 year.

7.3 Mutual Fund Complete Guide
What is a mutual fund? "Your money + others' money = managed by a professional fund manager." You do not buy stocks directly, the fund manager buys them. You get units. Fund performs well = your NAV increases = profit.
Types Detailed:
Fund Type Invests In Risk Return Range Best For
Equity Fund Stocks High 12-15% Long-term growth (7+ years)
Debt Fund Bonds/Govt securities Low 6-8% Short-term (1-3 years)
Hybrid Fund Equity + Debt mix Medium 8-10% Balanced approach
Index Fund Market index (Nifty 50) Medium 10-12% Beginners — low fee
ELSS Equity + tax saving Medium-High 12-15% Tax saving under 80C, 3yr lock-in
Liquid Fund Short-term debt Very Low 5-6.5% Emergency fund parking
Overnight Fund 1-day securities Ultra Low 4-5% Very short-term parking
Arbitrage Fund Equity arbitrage Low 6-8% Tax-efficient, 1+ year
Direct vs Regular Plan:
In direct plan expense ratio is lower (0.5-1% less), so returns are higher. In the long-term this difference is very large. Always choose DIRECT plan.
Example: ₹5,000/month SIP for 20 years.
- Regular plan (1.5% expense, ~10.5% net): Corpus ≈ ₹41 lakh
- Direct plan (0.5% expense, ~11.5% net): Corpus ≈ ₹47 lakh
- Difference ≈ ₹6 lakh! Just due to expense ratio.
Expense Ratio: Fee for managing the fund. Should be low. In index funds it is 0.1-0.3% (lowest), in active funds 1-2% (higher).
NAV: Net Asset Value — price of one unit. Changes daily.
AUM: Assets Under Management — total money in the fund. Higher AUM = stable fund, but very high AUM = difficult to manage (especially mid/small cap).
Rating: Check CRISIL, Morningstar ratings. 5-star = historically good, but not a past performance guarantee.

7.5 PPF — Public Provident Fund
PPF is a government scheme — 15 year lock-in, tax-free return, currently 7.1% interest (Q2 FY 2026-27, unchanged since April 2020 — 6+ years). Minimum ₹500/year, maximum ₹1.5 lakh/year. EEE status = Exempt-Exempt-Exempt — tax deduction on investment, tax-free interest, tax-free maturity. Where to open: Bank or Post Office.

Premature Withdrawal Rules:
After 5 years, partial withdrawal allowed under specific conditions. Maximum 50% of balance. For education, medical emergency.
Loan Against PPF:
Loan allowed between 3rd to 6th year. 25% of balance. Interest 1% above PPF rate. Repayment within 36 months.
PPF for Students: Long-term tax-saving tool. Start at age 18, maturity at 33 = ₹16 lakh+ (₹5,000/month). If started at 25 then at 40 = same amount. Early start = magic.
7.6 Stock Market Basics
What is stock market? It is a marketplace where shares of companies are bought and sold. Share = small ownership piece of a company. If company does well, share price rises — you earn profit. BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange) and NSE (National Stock Exchange) — 2 main exchanges of India. Sensex = index of top 30 companies of BSE. Nifty 50 = index of top 50 companies of NSE.
Demat Account:
Account to hold shares in digital form. How to open: Zerodha, Groww, Upstox — online in 10 minutes. Charges: Account opening free (usually), AMC ₹300/year, brokerage 0.03% or flat ₹20.
Is it Safe for Beginners — Honest Answer:
Direct stock picking is risky for beginners. Better option: Index fund — invest in Nifty 50 index fund, you will get market average return. Picking individual stocks requires research and experience. 90% of retail investors are unable to beat the market.
Index Fund is Better than Individual Stocks — 5 Reasons:
Diversification — 50 companies together. One company fails = no problem.
Professional management — fund manager tracks it.
Low cost — expense ratio 0.1% vs brokerage + research cost.
No emotional decisions — SIP runs automatically.
Historically consistent — Nifty 50 long-term ~12%.
10 Golden Rules If You Want to Enter Stock Market:
First make an emergency fund.
First start SIP in an index fund.
Keep separate capital for direct stocks — "fun money" that you can afford 100% loss.
Never buy stocks with borrowed money.
Do not put >10% capital on 1 stock.
Invest for long-term — not trading.
Do not do F&O (Futures & Options) at all as a beginner — 90% retail investors incur loss.
Do not invest without research — read annual report, balance sheet.
Invest only if you can afford loss.
Do not put money on "hot tips" — WhatsApp forward ≠ research.
Fundamental Analysis Basics (Simple Introduction):
PE Ratio: Price to Earnings. How many years will it take to recover price from company profits. 15 = reasonable, 30 = expensive, <10 = cheap (maybe).
Market Cap: Total value of company. Large cap = safe, small cap = risky.
Debt: How much loan company has. Low debt = healthy.
ROE: Return on Equity. 15%+ = good management.
Detailed stock analysis is not in Module 8, take an advanced course or consult an advisor.

7.8 Crypto — One Honest Section
What is Crypto? Digital currency based on blockchain technology. Bitcoin, Ethereum — most popular. But 5 strict warnings for students:
80% crypto projects fail — long-term survival uncertain. Out of top 100 coins of 2017, 80 are now zero.
No regulation in India — if fraud happens no one will protect. Exchange shut down = money gone.
30% tax on gains + 1% TDS — almost one-third of returns goes in tax. Plus 4% cess.
Extreme volatility — 50% up or 50% down possible in a week. ₹1 lakh → ₹50,000 in 3 days.
Scams are very common — fraud exchanges, rug pulls, fake coins. "Guaranteed 10x" = 100% scam.
Recommendation for Students: First strengthen basics — FD, SIP, PPF. Crypto LATER, if ever, and only as much as you can afford 100% loss. Crypto ≠ investing, crypto = speculation. Understand the difference.
7.9 Asset Allocation for Students
Asset allocation = dividing money among different assets. Ideal allocation for Age 18-25:
Asset Percentage Where Why
Equity (Growth) 60% SIP in Index Fund Young age = high risk capacity, long time horizon
Debt (Stability) 20% FD/RD/Liquid Fund Safe returns, emergency fund base
Gold (Hedge) 10% SGB/Digital Gold Inflation hedge, diversification
Cash (Emergency) 10% Savings Account Instant access for emergencies
Aggressive vs Conservative:
Aggressive (Age 18-22): 70% equity, 15% debt, 10% gold, 5% cash. Time horizon 40 years.
Moderate (Age 23-25): 60% equity, 20% debt, 10% gold, 10% cash. First job, want stability.
Conservative (Age 26+): 50% equity, 25% debt, 15% gold, 10% cash. Family planning starts.
Rebalancing:
Check every 6-12 months. Equity grew too much (became 70% from target 60%)? Take out a little from equity and put into debt. This controls risk.
7.10 Tax Basics for Students (Cross-reference Module 10)
Quick overview:
- Income tax slab: 0-₹4L = no tax (new regime). Income up to ₹12L effectively tax-free due to Section 87A rebate (₹60,000). Salaried individuals get ₹75,000 standard deduction (up to ₹12.75L tax-free). Most students earn less than this.
- Section 80C: Deduction up to ₹1.5L — ELSS, PPF, 5yr FD.
- Section 80CCD: Extra ₹50K deduction in NPS.
- Section 80TTA: Savings interest tax-free up to ₹10K.
- Section 80E: Education loan interest — full deduction, no limit, 8 years.
- Form 16: Employer gives — salary + TDS summary.
- ITR: File even if zero tax — proof for refund, loan, visa.
