# Module 10: TAX BASICS FOR STUDENTS

MODULE 10: TAX BASICS FOR STUDENTS
10.1 What is Income Tax — Why Do We Have to Pay It
Income tax is the money that the government deducts from your earnings (income). This is a contribution to run the country — roads, hospitals, schools, defense all run from this. In India, income tax is a direct tax — meaning it is cut directly from your earnings. In every financial year (April 1 to March 31) your total income is calculated, and tax is levied on it based on "slabs" — higher income = higher tax.
Important question for students: "Will I have to pay tax?"
Answer: If your annual income is less than ₹2.5 lakh (old regime) or less than ₹4 lakh (new regime), then you have to pay ZERO tax. And in the new regime, through Section 87A rebate, tax becomes ZERO even on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh — salary up to ₹12.75 lakh (after ₹75,000 standard deduction taxable is ₹12 lakh) = nil tax.
Income of most students is less than this, so formally tax does not have to be paid. But filing ITR is still beneficial — for refund, for loan, for visa.

Important: On income up to ₹5 lakh tax becomes zero through Section 87A rebate (old regime). In new regime, through 87A rebate ZERO tax on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh (from FY 2025-26). Student's salary ₹25,000/month = ₹3 lakh/year = effectively zero tax. But do file ITR!
Marginal Relief Note: If taxable income is slightly more than ₹12 lakh (like ₹12.1L-₹12.3L zone), there is no need to worry. Through marginal relief your tax payable will not be more than the income above ₹12 lakh. Meaning tax on ₹12.1L will remain limited to only ₹10,000 — full slab tax of ₹61,500 is not levied.
10.2 Important Tax Sections for Students — Detailed with Examples
Government has provided some sections for tax savings that are relevant for students:
Section 80C:
Tax deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh on investment. ELSS (SIP), PPF, FD (5-year), NSC, insurance premium — all come under this.
Example: ₹3 lakh taxable income, ₹1.5 lakh ELSS invested = taxable income ₹1.5 lakh = zero tax! (In old regime).
Section 80CCD:
Additional ₹50,000 deduction in NPS (National Pension Scheme). If doing long-term retirement planning, NPS + 80CCD = extra tax saved. Total 80C + 80CCD = ₹2 lakh deduction.
Section 80TTA/80TTB:
Deduction up to ₹10,000 on savings account interest. On FD interest for senior citizens ₹50,000 (80TTB). For students savings interest is a small but useful deduction.
Example: Received ₹15,000 interest in savings account. ₹10,000 exempt through 80TTA. Taxable interest = ₹5,000 only.
Section 80GG:
Living on rent and not receiving HRA? Deduction up to ₹60,000/year (conditions apply). PG/Hostel rent is also covered. Form 10BA has to be filled.
Example: ₹5,000/month rent = ₹60,000/year. ₹60,000 deduction through 80GG. Taxable income reduced.
Section 80E:
Deduction on interest of education loan — no limit! Can claim up to 8 years. When EMI starts, take deduction of interest portion.
Example: ₹8 lakh education loan at 9%. First year interest = ₹72,000. Full ₹72,000 deductible through 80E. Taxable income directly reduced by ₹72,000.
10.3 What is Form 16 — How to Read It
Form 16 is the document given by your employer that shows how much was your salary throughout the year, how much TDS was deducted, and how much tax you paid. This is essential for filing ITR.
Form 16 has 2 parts:
Part A: Details of employer and TDS info. TAN number, PAN, total TDS deposited.
Part B: Salary breakdown, deductions, and tax calculation. Basic, HRA, LTA, deductions (80C, 80D), taxable income, tax payable.
How to Read:
Check gross salary — this is your total income.
See exemptions — HRA, LTA, standard deduction (old regime: ₹50,000; new regime: ₹75,000 FY 2025-26).
Verify deductions — 80C, 80D, 80E that you claimed.
Taxable income = Gross - Exemptions - Deductions.
Tax calculated = final tax liability.
TDS = tax already paid. If TDS > tax liability = refund. If TDS < tax = tax payable.
Ask employer for Form 16 in April-June after getting a job — this is your right. Learn to read Form 16 — it states where government put your money and how many deductions were claimed.
10.4 ITR Filing — Why and How
ITR (Income Tax Return) is the form you fill to give details of your annual income and tax to the government. Even if your income is below taxable limit, filing ITR is beneficial.
4 Reasons to File ITR Even with Zero Tax:
To claim tax refund — if TDS was deducted and income is not taxable, you will get refund. ₹2,000 TDS = ₹2,000 refund.
For loan approval — bank checks ITR. Home loan, car loan, personal loan — ITR = income proof.
For visa — ITR proof is required for foreign travel. US, UK, Schengen — all ask for ITR.
For financial record — official income history. Useful if starting a business in future.
ITR Filing Process — 7 Steps:
Step 1: Go to incometax.gov.in (official website).
Step 2: Register with PAN (would already be registered if done earlier).
Step 3: Select ITR-1 (for salary income) or ITR-2 (for capital gains).
Step 4: Fill income details from Form 16.
Step 5: Claim deductions under 80C, 80D, 80E etc.
Step 6: Verify tax calculation — payable or refund.
Step 7: Verify with Aadhaar OTP or digital signature. Submit!
It is free — no CA needed for simple salaried individuals. Time: 15-30 minutes.
Deadline: July 31 of every year (for previous financial year). Late filing = ₹1,000-₹5,000 penalty.
ITR-1 vs ITR-2 vs ITR-3:
ITR-1 (Sahaj): Salary + one house property + other sources (interest). Most students/freshers.
ITR-2: Capital gains (SIP, stocks), multiple house property. If equity was sold.
ITR-3: Business/profession income. Freelancers, business owners.
ITR-4 (Sugam): Presumptive income. Freelancers with 44ADA.
Common ITR Mistakes:
Selecting wrong ITR form.
TDS mismatch — Form 16 vs Form 26AS.
Overclaiming deductions — fake 80C receipts.
Not declaring bank interest — will get caught by AI.
Not verifying — ITR wasn't filed at all!
10.5 Student-Specific Tax Situations
FD Interest Tax:
On interest received from FD, TDS of 10% is deducted (on exceeding ₹40,000/year). If total income is not taxable, submit Form 15G — TDS will not be deducted.
Example: ₹50,000 FD interest. Bank will cut ₹5,000 TDS. Form 15G submitted → TDS zero. Refund also not required.
SIP Returns Tax:
Holding equity fund for more than 1 year Long Term Capital Gain (LTCG) is tax-free up to ₹1.25 lakh. On more than that 12.5% tax.
On debt funds tax at your slab rate. On debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 ALL gains are considered short-term capital gains — tax at slab rate, no matter how many years held. (Old rule for funds bought before 1 April 2023: held for more than 3 years = 20% with indexation.)
Example: ₹2 lakh LTCG equity. ₹1.25 lakh exempt. 12.5% on ₹75,000 = ₹9,375 tax.
Freelance Income Tax:
Freelance income is also taxable! Tax on ₹4L+ (new regime). 50% presumptive deduction is available under 44ADA. Meaning declare ₹6 lakh income, 50% = ₹3 lakh automatic deduction. Taxable = ₹3 lakh. No receipts needed!
Example: Freelance income ₹8 lakh. 44ADA: 50% deduction = ₹4 lakh. Taxable = ₹4 lakh. Tax = ZERO (new regime — nil slab up to ₹4L + 87A rebate).
Stipend Tax:
Stipend is taxable (under salary).
Scholarship is tax-free (Section 10(16)).
PhD stipend = taxable (if it's employment).
Government scholarship = tax-free.
Verify: Stipend = in exchange for services? Taxable. Scholarship = merit/need based? Tax-free.
10.6 What is TDS — Tax Deducted at Source
TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) means — tax gets cut even before money arrives. Like FD interest is ₹50,000, bank will cut ₹5,000 TDS, you will get ₹45,000. This is advance tax — government wants money right now, not later.
To Check TDS:
Form 26AS: On incometax.gov.in — shows all TDS details. Updated every quarter.
AIS (Annual Information Statement): New system — detailed income breakdown. Banks, employers, mutual funds all report.
If extra TDS was deducted, file ITR and take refund! Refund comes in bank account in 3-6 months.
Common TDS Scenarios:
Salary: Employer cuts TDS (as per Form 16).
FD interest: 10% TDS above ₹40,000/year (₹50,000 for senior citizens).
Freelance: Client cuts 10% TDS above ₹30,000/year (Section 194J — per financial year, not per transaction).
Rent: Tenant 5% TDS above ₹50,000/month.
10.7 Old Regime vs New Regime — Which is Better for Students
Comparison:
Factor Old Regime New Regime
Basic exemption ₹2.5L ₹4L
87A rebate (zero tax) Up to ₹5L income Up to ₹12L income
Deductions (80C etc.) Allowed Most NOT allowed
Standard deduction ₹50,000 ₹75,000
Tax rates Higher slabs Lower slabs
Rule of Thumb for Students:
If you are investing in 80C (SIP, PPF, FD) → Old Regime is better because taxable income reduces from deductions.
If no investment is being done → New Regime is better because lower tax rates are there + higher exemption.
Calculator Example with ₹6 lakh salary:
Old Regime: ₹6,00,000 - ₹50,000 (standard) - ₹1,50,000 (80C ELSS) = ₹4,00,000 taxable. Tax = ₹7,500. 87A rebate = ₹7,500. Tax = ZERO!
New Regime: ₹6,00,000 - ₹75,000 = ₹5,25,000 taxable. Tax = ₹6,250 (5% above ₹4L). 87A rebate (up to ₹12L) = ₹6,250. Tax = ZERO!
Zero tax in both. But if ₹1.5 lakh invested in 80C → habit formed + wealth created in Old regime. No investment in New regime = no wealth.
Decision Flowchart:
Are you investing in 80C?
    ↓ YES → Old Regime (deductions + lower taxable income)
    ↓ NO → Is income less than ₹12L?
        ↓ YES → New Regime (lower rates, no deductions needed)
        ↓ NO → Calculate both, choose whichever gives lower tax
Calculator: Free calculator available on incometax.gov.in. Compare both.

Recommendation for Students:
ELSS ₹500-₹1,000/month — 3-year lock-in, best returns, tax saving. Start it.
PPF ₹500/year minimum — 15-year lock-in, long-term base. Open it, put minimum.
NPS optional — If on job employer contribution is free money. Optional in student life.
5-year FD — Conservative, safe. But returns low, barely beat inflation.
WHERE PEOPLE MAKE MISTAKES (Module 10)
Not filing ITR thinking "income is low" — Refund is getting missed, will cause problem in loan/visa. File it!
Choosing new regime without calculation — If 80C deductions are there then old regime is better. Calculate first.
Not claiming refund after TDS deduction — Submit Form 15G or file ITR. Do not leave money behind.
Considering freelance income tax-free — This is fully taxable. Use 44ADA, but declare it.
Not claiming deduction for education loan interest (80E) — No upper limit for this deduction. Full interest deductible. Do not miss it.
Not learning to read Form 16 — Employer can cut wrong TDS. Verify it. Form 16 = your right.
Missing July 31 deadline — Late filing penalty ₹1,000-₹5,000 (income below ₹5L: ₹1,000; otherwise up to ₹5,000). Mark in calendar.
Doing tax planning only "later" — Start from April. Will not be able to put ₹1.5 lakh together in March. Monthly tax planning through SIP.
KEY TAKEAWAYS (Module 10)
₹2.5L (old)/₹4L (new) basic exemption, and in new regime 87A rebate taxable income up to ₹12L (salary up to ₹12.75L) = zero tax — most students don't have to pay tax. But file ITR.
File ITR even with zero tax — essential for refund, loan approval, visa. 15 minutes online.
Save tax up to ₹1.5L from Section 80C — ELSS is best for students (3yr lock-in, 12-15% returns).
Check TDS in Form 26AS — take refund if cut extra. Stop TDS through Form 15G.
Take full deduction of education loan interest from 80E — no limit. Claim up to 8 years.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS (Module 10)
"Student has nothing to do with tax" — Wrong! Freelance income, stipend, FD interest all are taxable. Awareness needed.
"ITR filing is complicated" — No! ITR-1 gets filled online in 15 minutes, it is free. Step-by-step guide available.
"New regime is always better" — No! If claiming 80C/80D/80E deductions then old regime is better. Calculate it.
"Tax planning is only for rich people" — Wrong! Tax of ₹750 can be saved from ₹5,000 ELSS. Every rupee counts.
"Work is over once Form 16 is received" — No! Verify Form 16, file ITR, claim refund. Complete the process.
