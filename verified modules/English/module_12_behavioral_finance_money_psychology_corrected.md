## 12.1 What Is Behavioral Finance — Introduction

Brother, in Module 1 we saw what money is and how it works. But wait — have you ever thought about why you still go shopping even when you know you should save? Why do you use a credit card even when you know it's dangerous? This is the magic of **Behavioral Finance**!

### Traditional Finance vs Behavioral Finance

| Feature | Traditional Finance | Behavioral Finance |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Assumption | Humans are rational | Humans are emotional + rational |
| Decision making | Done using logic | Feelings also affect decisions |
| Market behavior | Perfect information exists | Information is incomplete |
| Goal | Maximum profit | Understand why you make mistakes |
| Example | "Calculate ROI" | "Why aren't you able to calculate ROI?" |

### Why Do Smart People Make Dumb Money Decisions?

Look, our brain works using two systems:

1. **System 1 (Fast Brain)** — Works automatically, emotionally, without thinking
2. **System 2 (Slow Brain)** — Works thoughtfully, logically

The problem is that **in money decisions, our System 1 is more active**. When you enter a mall and see a "50% OFF" board, you immediately feel like buying — this is System 1. When you think "do I really need this?" — this is System 2.

**Real Example:**
Rahul knows his monthly budget is ₹5,000. But when the "Great Indian Festival" comes on Amazon, he thinks — "Man, when will I get such a discount again!" and spends ₹15,000. Later, seeing the credit card bill, he goes into depression.

### India's Financial Literacy Status

| Statistic | Data |
|-----------|------|
| Financial Literacy Rate | 27% (only!) |
| People who invest in stock market | ~5% of population |
| Credit card default rate | ~3-4% annually |
| Average savings rate | Declining from 30% to 18% |
| People with emergency fund | Less than 15% |

### Emotional Brain vs Logical Brain — The Real Battle

```
EMOTIONAL BRAIN (Limbic System)          LOGICAL BRAIN (Prefrontal Cortex)
┌─────────────────────────┐              ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ "Sale is on! Buy it!"   │              │ "Do I need this?"       │
│ "Everyone is buying"    │              │ "Check the budget"      │
│ "Feeling good today"    │              │ "Can you afford EMI?"   │
│ "Getting FOMO"          │              │ "Think of opportunity"  │
└─────────────────────────┘              └─────────────────────────┘
        90% decisions                        10% decisions
```

This is the core concept of **Behavioral Finance** — our brain makes mistakes in money decisions, and we should understand why.

## 12.3 Cognitive Biases That Make You Helpless

Brother, this is the most important section. **Cognitive biases** are glitches in our brain that force us into wrong decisions. Understand these, and life becomes easy!

### 12 Cognitive Biases — Detailed Breakdown

#### 1. Anchoring Bias

**Definition:** The first number you see becomes an anchor and all subsequent decisions are affected by it.

**How it affects money:**
Calculating discount by looking at MRP — "It was ₹1000, getting it for ₹500, 50% off!" But did you really need a ₹500 item?

**Indian Example:**

| Product | MRP | Selling Price | "Discount" | Actual Value |
|---------|-----|---------------|------------|--------------|
| T-shirt | ₹1,999 | ₹999 | 50% off! | ₹300 worth fabric |
| Watch | ₹5,000 | ₹2,500 | 50% off! | ₹800 worth mechanism |
| Headphones | ₹3,000 | ₹1,499 | 50% off! | ₹600 worth sound |

Priya bought a "₹5,000 bag" for ₹2,000. She felt she saved a lot. But in reality, the bag was worth ₹800 — even ₹2,000 was overpriced!

**How to overcome:**
- Think: "Would I buy this item without a discount?"
- Calculate actual value, not discount
- Ignore the word "Discount" — look at the price, not the percentage

---

#### 2. Loss Aversion

**Definition:** A loss feels 2 to 2.5 times worse than the pleasure of an equivalent gain.

**How it affects money:**
- Losing ₹100 feels 2.5x worse, whereas gaining ₹100 doesn't feel as good
- Because of this, people avoid risky investments even if expected return is high
- Because of this, people don't sell stocks at a loss and keep holding them

**Indian Example:**
Amit invested ₹10,000 in the stock market. Price rose to ₹12,000 (₹2,000 profit). Then it dropped to ₹9,000 (₹1,000 loss). Now he is holding because "selling will confirm the loss." Later it drops to ₹6,000 — a real loss of ₹4,000!

**The Math:**
| Scenario | Feeling | Action |
|----------|---------|--------|
| ₹100 profit | 😊 Happy (level 1) | Let's sell! |
| ₹100 loss | 😢 Very sad (level 2.5) | Let's hold... |

**How to overcome:**
- Pre-decide: "I will sell at a 10% loss"
- Look at the portfolio, not a single stock
- Don't make emotional decisions — make rules

---

#### 3. Herd Mentality

**Definition:** Doing what everyone else is doing, without thinking.

**How it affects money:**
- Everyone is buying an iPhone, so I will buy one too
- Everyone is investing in crypto, so I will too
- Everyone is buying a flat, so I will too

**Indian Example:**

| Year | Trend | Result |
|------|-------|--------|
| 2021 | Crypto boom | People invested after taking loans; 80% lost money |
| 2022 | Real estate boom | Prices rose even in small cities; buyers got stuck |
| 2023 | IPO craze | Many IPOs flop, retail investors in loss |
| 2024 | AI stocks | Valuations overinflated |

Kunal invested ₹50,000 in an IPO along with all his friends. Everyone said "this is a rocket!" On IPO listing, it came down by 20%. After 6 months, it is 40% down. Kunal's ₹50,000 remains at ₹30,000.

**How to overcome:**
- Think: "Would I have made this decision alone?"
- Research the asset, not the trend
- Think contrarian — "if everyone is buying, it might be overpriced"

---

#### 4. Confirmation Bias

**Definition:** Reading only that information which supports your existing belief.

**How it affects money:**
- You bought a stock — now you only read positive news about it
- You feel real estate is best — you only look at real estate benefits
- You are against credit cards — you don't look at credit card benefits

**Indian Example:**
Rahul invested ₹1 lakh in a startup. Now he only reads positive articles about that startup. Seeing negative reviews, he thinks "these people are jealous." After 6 months, the startup shut down. Rahul knew, but he didn't face reality.

**How to overcome:**
- Intentionally read opposite views
- Ask a friend to challenge your decision
- Maintain a decision journal (in Module 12.9)

---

#### 5. Recency Bias

**Definition:** Assuming that what happened recently will also happen in the future.

**How it affects money:**
- Last month stock market went up — thinking it will also go up in the future
- Rent has been rising for the past 3 years — thinking it will always rise
- A scheme gave profit for 3 months — assuming it will always yield profit

**Indian Example:**
Mutual Fund X gave a 25% annual return in the last 3 years. Priya thought "I'm getting 25%!" and invested ₹2 lakh. Next year market correction came, fund gave -10%. Priya was shocked — "this was not possible!"

**How to overcome:**
- Look at historical average, not recent performance
- Diversify (Module 7 cross-reference)
- Remember: "Past performance is not indicative of future results"

---

#### 6. Endowment Effect

**Definition:** Overvaluing an item simply because you own it.

**How it affects money:**
- Selling an old phone feels difficult even if you want a new one
- Selling your shares feels difficult even if they are overvalued
- Selling an old course feels difficult even if you are not using it

**Indian Example:**
Amit has an old laptop worth ₹15,000 in the market. He thinks "it cost ₹40,000, I will sell it for at least ₹25,000." But nobody will pay more than ₹15,000. He fails to sell it and cannot buy a new laptop.

**How to overcome:**
- Think: "If I didn't own this, how much would I buy it for?"
- Accept market price
- Separate emotion from reality

---

#### 7. Sunk Cost Fallacy

**Definition:** "I have already invested so much money and time, I cannot leave now."

**How it affects money:**
- Movie is boring but watching because "paid money for it"
- Course is useless but continuing because "invested ₹10,000"
- Stock is in loss but "will average down"

**Indian Example:**

| Scenario | Sunk Cost | Right Decision |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| Boring movie | ₹200 ticket | Leave the movie; ₹200 is already gone |
| Useless course | ₹10,000 fee | Quit the course; ₹10,000 is already gone |
| Bad stock | ₹50,000 invested | Sell; ₹50,000 is already gone |
| Bad relationship | 3 years invested | Move on; 3 years are already gone |

Sneha paid ₹8,000 for an online course. After watching 2 lectures, she realized the course was useless. But she thought "money is gone, learn something at least." Wasted 2 months and that too was useless.

**How to overcome:**
- Think: "If this were offered for free right now, what would I do?"
- Evaluate future cost-benefit, not past
- Accept "money is gone" and move on

---

#### 8. Present Bias

**Definition:** Today's comfort feels more important than tomorrow's benefit.

**How it affects money:**
- Party today, save tomorrow
- Buy today, pay EMI tomorrow
- Enjoy today, invest tomorrow

**Indian Example:**
Kunal's monthly budget:
| Item | Budgeted | Actual | Reason |
|------|----------|--------|--------|
| Food | ₹3,000 | ₹5,000 | "Had to go out with friends today" |
| Entertainment | ₹1,000 | ₹3,000 | "New movie released" |
| Shopping | ₹2,000 | ₹6,000 | "There was a sale, man!" |
| Savings | ₹4,000 | ₹0 | "Definitely from tomorrow!" |

**Calculation:**
If Kunal saved ₹4,000 every month and got a 12% return:
| Duration | Amount Saved | With Returns |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 year | ₹48,000 | ₹50,730 |
| 5 years | ₹2,40,000 | ₹3,26,679 |
| 10 years | ₹4,80,000 | ₹9,21,024 |

Now Kunal is taking "today's fun", but losing ₹9.21 lakh in the future!

**How to overcome:**
- Set up auto-debit (Module 12.7)
- Apply "Pay yourself first" rule
- Put visual reminders — "What will happen after 10 years?"

---

#### 9. Mental Accounting

**Definition:** Dividing money into different categories that does not make logical sense.

**How it affects money:**
- Saving from salary but spending away bonus
- Having money in one account while taking a loan in another
- Spending cash more freely than card

**Indian Example:**
Meera got ₹20,000 in bonus. She thought "this is extra, I can spend it." But actually she had ₹15,000 due on her credit card. If she paid the ₹15,000 credit card due (saving 24% interest), it would be a much smarter decision.

**Mental Accounting Mistakes:**

| Category | Mental Account | Actual Impact |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| Salary | "This is regular, will save" | Often not saved |
| Bonus | "This is extra, will enjoy" | Often wasted |
| Gift money | "This is a gift, will spend" | Often wasted |
| Cash | "This is right here, will get spent" | Often spent frivolously |
| Credit | "This is not right now, will pay later" | Debt increases |

**How to overcome:**
- Treat all money as equal — whether salary or bonus
- Clear high-interest debt first
- "One pot" thinking — all money is in one place

---

#### 10. Framing Effect

**Definition:** Decisions change based on how information is presented.

**How it affects money:**
- "95% success rate" vs "5% failure rate" — both are the same, but the first feels more attractive
- "Free delivery" vs "₹50 delivery charge" — total cost can be the same in both

**Indian Example:**

| Framing | Option A | Option B | Same Thing? |
|---------|----------|----------|-------------|
| Insurance | "₹10 lakh coverage for ₹5,000/year" | "₹417/month for peace of mind" | Yes! |
| SIP | "Invest ₹1,000/month" | "Save ₹12,000/year for future" | Yes! |
| Credit Card | "3% cashback on all purchases" | "Pay 2.5% less on everything" | Similar! |

**How to overcome:**
- Look at numbers, not framing
- Frame the same information in different ways
- Ignore emotional language

---

#### 11. Availability Bias

**Definition:** What is easily remembered or seen feels more probable or common.

**How it affects money:**
- Seeing plane crash news makes flying feel dangerous (road accidents are actually far higher)
- Hearing a friend's stock loss makes stock market feel risky
- Seeing one successful friend makes it look like everyone is successful

**Indian Example:**
Saw news of a ₹100 crore bank fraud. Now it feels like "fraud happens in all banks!" But actual data: Indian banking fraud rate is < 0.1%.

| Risk | Perceived Risk | Actual Risk |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Plane crash | Very high! | 1 in 11 million |
| Road accident | "It's fine" | 1 in 5,000 |
| Stock market loss | "Will lose everything" | 10% long-term negative |
| Bank fraud | "Everyone is a thief" | < 0.1% |

**How to overcome:**
- Look at statistics, not anecdotes
- Consider the base rate
- Ask "How many times has this actually happened?"

---

#### 12. Overconfidence Effect

**Definition:** Overestimating one's own ability.

**How it affects money:**
- "I will earn 50% from stock market" — professionals can't even earn 15% consistently
- "I can time the market" — 90% of traders lose
- "Fraud won't happen to me" — everyone thinks so

**Indian Example:**
Amit (IIT Delhi) thinks "I am intelligent, I will easily earn in the stock market." He invested ₹50,000, remaining with ₹30,000 in 6 months. He thinks "luck was bad." But reality: 90% retail traders lose.

**Data:**

| Trader Type | Success Rate | Average Return |
|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| Professional traders | 10% [NEEDS_HUMAN_REVIEW] | 15-20% [NEEDS_HUMAN_REVIEW] |
| Retail traders | 5-10% [NEEDS_HUMAN_REVIEW — SEBI data: 93% lose in F&O, 71% lose in intraday equity] | -5% to 5% |
| Long-term investors | 80%+ | 10-12% |

**How to overcome:**
- Develop humility — "I could be wrong too"
- Take professional advice
- Track your decisions (decision journal)

## 12.6 Money Scripts — Hidden Programs In Your Mind

Brother, there are "programs" about money in our minds running since childhood. These are **Money Scripts** — unconscious beliefs about money.

### What Are Money Scripts?

Money Scripts are deeply held beliefs we learned from parents, culture, and experiences. They work automatically without us knowing.

**Example:**
- Father used to say "money doesn't grow on trees" → you still think "earning money is hard"
- Mother used to say "keep saving" → you still hoard money
- Family said "rich people are thieves" → you are scared of becoming rich

### 4 Money Script Types

#### 1. Money Avoidance (Money Is Bad)

**Core Belief:** "Money is bad," "People do anything for money," "Money doesn't bring happiness"

**Manifestations:**

| Behavior | Example | Impact |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Ignoring finances | Not checking bank balance | Unpaid bills, debt increases |
| Underearning | Not negotiating salary | Potential income loss |
| Giving away money | Donating all money | Personal financial crisis |
| Avoiding investments | Keeping everything in FD | Money depreciates due to inflation |

**Indian Example:**
Sneha's parents used to say "money is not everything." Sneha believed this. She doesn't negotiate salary, doesn't invest, doesn't check bank balance. "Money comes and goes." Reality: money is important for basic needs.

**How Money Avoidance Forms:**
- Seeing parents' financial struggle
- "There is no harm in being poor" culture
- Misinterpreted religious teachings
- Past financial trauma

---

#### 2. Money Worship (Money Is Everything)

**Core Belief:** "If you have money, you have everything," "More money, more happiness," "Money solves everything"

**Manifestations:**

| Behavior | Example | Impact |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Overworking | Working 80 hours/week | Health damage |
| Hoarding | Keeping money, not spending | Missing life experiences |
| Status seeking | Buying branded items | Debt, no savings |
| Relationship neglect | "Will leave everything for money" | Lonely later |

**Indian Example:**
Rahul's parents always said "earn money, everything else will be fine." Rahul now works 80 hours. Money is coming, but health is ruined, no relationships, no joy. He thinks "if I earn more, everything will be fine."

**How Money Worship Forms:**
- Parents' poverty struggle
- "Earning money = success" narrative
- Respect for rich people in society
- Materialism culture

---

#### 3. Money Status (Money = Respect)

**Core Belief:** "More money means more respect," "Rich people are better," "Show money, get respect"

**Manifestations:**

| Behavior | Example | Impact |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Status spending | Branded items just to show off | Debt |
| Comparison | Competing with friends | Never satisfied |
| Financial lying | Exaggerating income | Stress |
| Judgmental attitude | Looking down on poor people | Poor relationships |

**Indian Example:**
Kunal earns ₹15,000 but spends ₹25,000 to show off. Branded clothes, expensive phone, restaurant meals. "People should know I earn." Has a ₹50,000 debt on credit cards.

**How Money Status Forms:**
- Social media comparison
- Glamorization of rich people in Bollywood
- Parents' status obsession
- Peer pressure

---

#### 4. Money Vigilance (Save Money, Save Money)

**Core Belief:** "Save money," "Don't spend," "Keep it for the future"

**Manifestations:**

| Behavior | Example | Impact |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Over-saving | Saving 80% of salary | Missing present joys |
| Frugality to extreme | Stinginess in everything | Poor quality of life |
| Financial anxiety | Constantly worrying about money | Mental health issues |
