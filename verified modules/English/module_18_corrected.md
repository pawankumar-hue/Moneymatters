### 18.1.1 REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) — Commercial Real Estate with ₹500

Imagine a mall or commercial IT park where giant companies like Google, Microsoft, TCS, Amazon, and HDFC Bank pay rent. The price of that entire commercial complex is ₹5,000 Crore!

It is impossible for a normal middle-class student to buy an entire tech park. But a **REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust)** divides this entire tech park into lakhs of micro-shares (units).

#### Working Mechanism of REITs:
1. **Asset Pooling:** The REIT company (sponsor) buys or constructs Grade-A commercial properties (IT parks, shopping malls, corporate offices).
2. **Tenant Rent Collection:** Crores of rent is collected every month from corporate clients like Amazon, Google, Accenture.
3. **SEBI 90% Mandate Rule:** SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) has a strict rule that REITs HAVE TO distribute **90% of their Net Distributable Cash Flow (NDCF)** to their unit holders (investors)! SEBI's minimum mandate is that distribution must happen at least every 6 months (semi-annually), but most REITs in India voluntarily distribute quarterly (every 3 months).
4. **Unit Listing:** REITs trade on stock exchanges (NSE & BSE) just like shares. You can buy even 1 unit from your Demat Account (Zerodha, Groww, AngelOne)!

```
[Commercial Tech Parks / Malls] ──(Rent Payout)──> [REIT Entity]
                                                         │
                                           (SEBI Mandate: 90% NDCF Payout)
                                                         │
                                                         ▼
                                            [Unit Holders / Investors]
                                            (You and Me - Quarterly Income)
```

### 18.1.2 InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) — Investment in India's Backbone

If REITs are landlords of commercial real estate (offices, malls), then **InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts)** are owners of India's infrastructure highways, power transmission lines, gas pipelines, and renewable energy solar parks!

When you travel by car on an NHAI highway and Fastag is scanned at the Toll Plaza, or when electricity comes to your home through a Power Grid transmission line — a portion of that toll collection and transmission fee goes into the pockets of **InvIT investors**!

#### REITs vs InvITs Comparison:
- **REITs** rely on office rent & retail mall tenant sales.
- **InvITs** rely on government power transmission tariffs, highway toll collection, and long-term infrastructure contracts (often 15 to 30 years concession agreements).

#### Yield vs Growth Dynamics in InvITs:
Students should understand this point very carefully: **InvITs give High Cash Distribution Yield, but Capital Growth is Moderate.**

Why? Because highway concessions (e.g., a 20-year toll contract) expire over time (asset amortization). The InvIT manager has to acquire new highways or power lines before the old asset expires.
- **Ideal For:** Monthly/Quarterly cash flow generation (e.g., college expenses, pocket money, portfolio yield buffering).
- **Core Strategy:** Do not let quarterly distributions coming from InvITs stay silent — reinvest them into Nifty 50 Index funds or Core Equity portfolio! (See Section 18.3).

### 18.1.3 Secondary Market SGBs (Sovereign Gold Bonds) — The Hidden Goldmine

You read about Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) in Module 7:
- 8-year tenure bonds issued by RBI.
- 2.5% annual fixed interest payout (paid semi-annually).
- Capital gains tax 100% EXEMPT at maturity (tenor of 8 years, lock-in of 5 years)!

**But a major change occurred in 2024-2025:** Government of India and Reserve Bank of India have paused/halted fresh SGB issuances. The Government is now reducing or stopping new SGB issues due to high interest cost.

**So can students no longer buy SGBs? WRONG!**
You can still buy existing issued SGBs from the **Secondary Market** of NSE and BSE — and that too at a **3% to 8% DISCOUNT from the Fresh Issue Price!**

#### Why Are SGBs Available at a Discount in Secondary Market?
1. **Liquidity Need of Original Owners:** People who bought SGBs in 2019, 2020, or 2021 suddenly need cash in an emergency. Their 5-year lock-in (premature redemption window) has not been completed, or 8-year maturity is remaining.
2. **Low Trading Volume:** Regular retail buyers on the stock market do not know how to search secondary market SGB symbols (e.g., `SGBAUG28`, `SGBDE28`, `SGBMAY29I`). Sellers place sell orders at a price ₹200-₹400 per gram lower than market price for a fast exit!

#### Practical Math Example: Spot Gold vs Secondary SGB

Suppose today the market spot rate of 24 Karat Gold is **₹7,500 per gram**.

- **Option A: Physical Gold / Digital Gold**
  - Purchase Price: ₹7,500/g + 3% GST (₹225) + Making Charges (₹500) = **₹8,225/g**.
  - Annual Interest: ₹0.
  - Tax at Sale: Slab rate / LTCG tax applicable.

- **Option B: Secondary Market SGB on NSE (Symbol: `SGBMAY29VII`)**
  - NSE Traded Price: **₹7,150 per gram** (Discount of ₹350 from spot price!).
  - GST / Making Charge: ZERO!
  - Annual Interest: 2.5% p.a. on original issue price (credited to bank account every 6 months).
  - Capital Gains Tax at Maturity (May 2029): **ZERO TAX!**
